This analysis explores the relationship between market sentiment and trader behavior using Fear & Greed Index data combined with historical trading data.

The study reveals that traders behave differently depending on market sentiment. During Greed periods, traders show increased activity, larger trade sizes, and higher risk-taking behavior. However, this often leads to inconsistent returns due to overtrading.

In contrast, Fear periods are associated with lower trading activity and more cautious behavior, although profitability remains lower.

Segmentation analysis indicates that frequent traders tend to underperform compared to infrequent traders, highlighting the negative impact of overtrading.

Based on these findings, two actionable strategies are recommended: reducing leverage during Fear periods and limiting trade frequency during Greed periods to improve overall performance.

This analysis demonstrates how sentiment-driven insights can be used to design more disciplined and effective trading strategies.
